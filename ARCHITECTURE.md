# SafeGate: System Architecture

**What it is:** a compiler from hazards to a technical file. Test execution is a
plugin; the conformity evidence chain is the product.

**Who it is for:** manufacturers of driverless industrial trucks and AMRs who must
hold a valid conformity assessment under EN ISO 3691-4 and EN ISO 13849-1, and who
face Regulation (EU) 2023/1230, which applies from **20 January 2027**.

Version 0.4.0. Numbers in this document come from `docs/RESULTS.md`, which is
generated by `scripts/reproduce.py`. The T200 truck used as the worked example is
fictional.

---

## 1. The problem, stated precisely

A Notified Body assessor asks one question, recursively:

> Show me the evidence that this claim is true, and show me that the evidence
> was produced by the thing you say produced it.

In many organisations that question is answered with a Word document, a
requirements spreadsheet, a folder of PDF test reports and an engineer's memory of
which firmware build was on the truck in March. The chain from *hazard* to
*evidence* then exists only in people's heads, and these failures follow:

| Failure | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Report claims a PL the architecture does not achieve | recall, CE withdrawal |
| Test results from a superseded firmware build | re-test campaign |
| A failing run dropped before reporting | liability, fraud exposure |
| Requirement with no test case, found at audit | schedule slip |
| "It passed" with 2 mm of margin | field incident |

None of these are simulation problems. They are **bookkeeping problems with safety
consequences**, which is why a simulator does not solve them. (This framing is the
product hypothesis, not a measured market study; see section 13.)

### 1.1 Prior art and the build-vs-buy line

| Layer | Best available | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| OSC2 parsing, behaviour trees, simulation loop, Gazebo/ROS 2 plumbing | Intel Labs `scenario_execution` (arXiv:2409.07080, Apache-2.0) | **Use it.** SafeGate drives it through its step-based `SimulationInterface`. |
| Automotive scenario tooling | Foretellix, dSPACE, IPG CarMaker, Ansys medini | Scoped and priced for ISO 26262 and road vehicles, not ISO 3691-4 machinery. |
| Traceability, PL derivation, conformity policy, technical file | nothing found | **Build. This is the product.** |

## 2. Architectural principles

Six, in priority order.

**P1. Everything is content-addressed and immutable.** Node IDs are a pure function
of semantic content (`core/ids.py`), never `uuid4`, never autoincrement. Two
engineers authoring the same hazard on different branches produce the same ID.

**P2. The domain model is a typed graph, not a document.** Traceability is graph
reachability. "Is every hazard covered?" is a query, not a review meeting.

**P3. Derived values are never stored.** Performance Level is recomputed from the
architecture on every evaluation (`iso13849/pl.py`). So are PLr, coverage and the
required protective-field length.

**P4. Determinism or it is not evidence.** Five hashes (scenario, SUT build,
backend, configuration, seed) are part of a run's content ID. Seeds derive from
SHA-256, never from Python's per-process salted `hash()` (0.3.0 got this wrong, see
CHANGELOG). A backend that cannot reproduce declares `deterministic = False`; the
orchestrator runs every point three times and records disagreement as `FLAKY`, a
blocker.

**P5. Policy is data, not code.** Release criteria are negotiated between
engineering, the customer's safety department and the Notified Body. Policy as YAML
is reviewable and diffable; so are the pinned signer keys.

**P6. The report is a pure function of the evidence graph.** Nothing in the
technical file is hand-written, and the issued file's digest is recorded in the
chain so an edited copy does not verify.

+----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| L3  EXECUTION ADAPTERS       execution/adapter.py  sim/              |

Dependencies point downward. L4 does not import L5: the campaign takes a `derive`
callable, and the CLI wires in the stopping-budget signals. L3 knows nothing about
Performance Levels. The one pragmatic exception is the project loader, which parses
STL criteria at load time so a typo fails before a campaign starts.

all reachable hazards must not exceed the achieved PL.

## 5. Why STL and not assertions (L1)

A boolean assertion answers "did it pass?". STL's quantitative semantics returns a
real number rho:

| rho | meaning |
|---|---|
| > 0 | satisfied; rho is the **distance to violation** in the criterion's unit |
| < 0 | violated; \|rho\| is the **depth** of the violation |
| = 0 | on the boundary |

Consequences:

1. **Falsification becomes optimisation.** Minimising rho over the parameter space
   is a directed search for the worst case.
2. **Margin is reportable.** "Held with a minimum margin of 98 mm across 214 runs"
   is an argument an assessor can weigh; "214/214 passed" is not.
3. **Regression detection is sensitive.** A commit that cuts margin from 380 mm to
   15 mm passes every boolean test; `R-MARGIN-001` trips on it once the margin falls
   below the policy floor (20 mm in the examples).

For robustness to carry a physical meaning, the measured quantity has to be signed.
The SIL world reports `min_distance_to_person` as a signed distance, so a collision
yields its overlap depth instead of saturating at zero.

The surface syntax is small enough to teach in a short session. Unit suffixes
(`150ms`, `40mm`) are normalised, and linear expressions keep units intact:
`always speed <= zone_speed_limit + 0.05` has robustness in m/s.

Requirements usually talk about quantities no sensor publishes: "the field is at
least as long as the stopping budget requires at this speed", "muting never lasts
longer than 2 s". `iso3691/derived.py` computes those once, for every runner, as
the signals `required_field_length`, `muted_elapsed` and `separation_while_moving`.

**Performance, measured** (single thread, AMD EPYC 7R13; see RESULTS.md): unbounded
`always` over 10,000 samples is vectorised (about 0.05 ms). Bounded windows use a
monotonic-wedge O(n) loop in pure Python (about 10 ms per 10,000 samples). `Until`
is O(n^2) (about 90 ms at 1,000 samples) and rare in safety requirements. The 0.3.0
claim of a 4-minute gate over 10^5 traces of 10^4 samples was not measured; at
these speeds, bounded-window criteria on that volume take tens of minutes per core.

## 6. Three-phase campaign (L2 + L4)

| Phase | Sampler | Purpose | Default budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `BoundarySampler` | nominal, box corners (capped), face centres | 24 |
| 2 | `SobolSampler` | scrambled Sobol sweep; the coverage claim rests on it | 128 |
| 3 | `RobustnessGuidedSampler` | simulated annealing on rho with restarts | 64 |

Phases 1 and 2 run in parallel worker processes; results are recorded in sampling
order, so the evidence is identical for any worker count (tested). Phase 3 is
sequential and is skipped once a counterexample exists: one is enough to fail the
gate.

Coverage is reported as **two-way (pairwise) bin coverage**.

### Invariants the orchestrator will not let a caller override

- **I1. Everything executed is recorded**, including failures, errors, repeats and
  runs whose backend crashed.
- **I2. Non-deterministic backends get repeat execution**, and disagreement is
  `FLAKY`, not a pass.
- **I3. Partial execution is not a pass.** Any errored run, including a run whose
  worker process died, makes the test case `ERROR`. An interrupted campaign (no
  `campaign_end` entry) is a blocker.
- **Repeats count once.** Repeats of a point are tagged in the chain; the point's
  result does not depend on the order the repeats finished in.

## 7. Execution backends (L3)

| Runner | Tier | Deterministic | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SilRunner` | SIL | yes | the SafeGate 2D closed-loop world, in process (docs/SIMULATOR.md) |
| `ScenarioExecutionRunner` (simulation) | SIL | yes | the same world driven by Intel Labs `scenario_execution` 1.5.0 from OSC2 files, lock-step on its simulation clock |
| `Ros2Runner` | SIL | no (repeats each point) | the world as a ROS 2 Jazzy node in an isolated container, recorded with rosbag2 (MCAP) and extracted with a topic-to-signal mapping |
| `ScenarioExecutionRunner` (ros) | SIL | no | `ros2 launch scenario_execution_ros` against a site's Gazebo; uses the same bag extraction; not exercised here |
| `HilRunner` | HIL only if the rig declares `physical: true` | no | client for `safegate-hil/1` with contract checks (docs/HIL_PROTOCOL.md) |
| `ReplayRunner` | REPLAY | yes | recorded signals or rosbag2 directories, open loop |
| `NullRunner` | SIL | yes | closed-form point-mass smoke path |

The two SIL paths share scenario definitions: the in-process scripts and the OSC2
actions call the same `Actions` methods. Every scenario family is bit-identical
across the two (unit tests), and a full rev B campaign is compared run by run in
RESULTS.md.

A runner's tier is a property of the runner, never of the test case YAML. In 0.3.0
a test case declaring `tier: hil` turned simulator runs into HIL evidence.

## 8. Tamper evidence (L0)

| # | Threat | Control |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Careless mutation: a log overwritten, the report silently changes | Blobs are immutable, addressed by digest, `chmod 0444`. |
| T2 | **Selective reporting**: failing runs dropped | Append-only manifest; each entry commits to the previous one. The gate and report are built from the chain, not a directory listing. |
| T3 | **Wholesale rewrite or backdating**: the chain regenerated without failing runs and re-signed | Signatures are verified against keys pinned in the policy (`trusted_signers`), and the signing key lives outside the store. `anchor` writes a commitment to the head for publishing outside the store (signed git tag, RFC 3161); `verify --anchor` detects any rewrite of the anchored prefix. |
| T4 | Build or design-data mismatch | Pinning is inside the run ID; the gate and report read exactly one campaign; R-EVID-002 compares the campaign's project digest with the current design data and, if given, the expected build. |
| T5 | An issued report edited afterwards | The report's digest is recorded in the chain; `verify --report` recomputes the Merkle root and compares the file. |

Demonstrated on the rev A store (742 manifest entries, 316 failing runs; details in
RESULTS.md): deleting the failing runs, flipping one verdict and editing the issued
report all make `verify` fail. A chain rewritten without the failing runs and
re-signed with a fresh key **passes** a plain `verify`, and fails as soon as the
pinned key or the published anchor is used. That last pair is the reason T3 needs
controls outside the store; 0.3.0 had none.

The newest entry is the one linkage cannot protect, since nothing links to it yet.
An edit there is caught by the signature check, which runs against the key the
entry names even without pinned keys. If the editor re-signs with their own key,
only the pinned key or an anchor catches it (tested).

## 9. Policy rules shipped (L6)

| Rule | Severity | Enforces |
|---|---|---|
| `R-PL-001` | blocker | achieved PL >= PLr from the Annex A risk graph |
| `R-PL-002` | blocker | the PL determination itself is valid (CCF >= 65, channel count, admissible DC and MTTFd bands) |
| `R-TRACE-001` | blocker (major for dangling refs) | every hazard has a requirement |
| `R-TRACE-002` | blocker | every requirement has a test case |
| `R-TRACE-003` | major | every requirement has a machine-checkable criterion |
| `R-TRACE-004` | blocker | every requirement that covers a hazard is realised by a safety function |
| `R-EXEC-001` | blocker | no FAIL, FLAKY or ERROR, and the campaign ran to completion |
| `R-EXEC-002` | blocker | a test case verifying a PL d/e obligation needs HIL-tier evidence or above. The obligation is the highest of the required and achieved PL of the functions realising its requirements and the PLr of the hazards they cover |
| `R-EXEC-003` | blocker | the evidence tier meets the tier the test case declares |
| `R-COV-001` | major | two-way coverage above a threshold |
| `R-MARGIN-001` | major | worst-case margin above a floor |
| `R-EVID-001` | blocker / major | chain intact, every signature valid, consistent with anchors; untrusted signers are blockers; when the policy requires signed evidence (the default), unsigned entries and mixed signers are blockers and a signed chain with no pinned key is major (self-attested) |
| `R-EVID-002` | blocker | evidence produced against the current design data and the expected build |
| `R-ML-001` | blocker | ML in the safety path means the Notified Body route; checked even when the PL determination is invalid |

### On waivers

A waiver requires a **justification and a named approver**, the severity is **not
downgraded**, and every waived finding appears in the technical file.

### On `R-ML-001`

Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 Annex I Part A lists, as items 5 and 6, safety components
with fully or partially self-evolving behaviour using machine learning approaches
ensuring safety functions, and machinery embedding such systems. Part A products
need third-party conformity assessment by a Notified Body. ISO 13849-1 provides no
quantification route for such elements. The tool encodes this as a blocker rather
than a warning.

## 10. Deployment topology

```
Developer laptop                 CI runner                      Evidence store
----------------                 ---------                      --------------
safegate validate  --+
safegate pl          |      +--> safegate validate / pl
safegate fields      |      |    safegate run --signing-key $SECRET --workers N --+
                     |      |    safegate gate --expected-build $SHA  <-----------+  S3 / GCS / MinIO
git push ------------+------+    safegate report                                  |  (CAS blobs)
                                 safegate anchor -> signed git tag                 |
                                 safegate verify --trusted-key --report --anchor <-+  manifest.log
                                      |                                               (append-only,
                                      v                                                chained, signed)
                                merge blocked on blocker
                                                                     HIL rig farm (safegate-hil/1)
                                                                     nightly, PL d/e obligations

Notified Body receives: V-and-V.md, the evidence store, the pinned public key, the anchor
  -> safegate verify recomputes everything independently
```

A reference workflow for a firmware repository is in `docs/ci/safety-gate.yml`; this
repository's own CI is `.github/workflows/ci.yml`. The evidence store runs on a
directory or on S3-compatible object storage (`--store s3://bucket/prefix`), where
every manifest entry is its own object created with a conditional write, so storage
itself refuses overwrites and sequence collisions. Bucket Object Lock in compliance
mode adds deletion resistance on top of deletion detection.

## 11. Worked example: rev A found wanting, rev B as far as simulation can go

The examples are a fictional Category 3 tow tractor (T200 rev A) and the design
after its findings were addressed (rev B). Both run the same test ODD, except that
rev B's speed ranges (and zone limits) stop at its lower maximum speed, and rev B adds
a reversing test case.

### Rev A (`examples/amr_project`)

734 SIL runs, 4.7 s with 16 workers. Every test case fails, each for a different
design reason:

| Test case | Worst margin | What the counterexample says |
|---|---|---|
| TC-FIELD-001 | -2.635 m | The field table was sized with the 0.3.0 budget, whose approach term ignored stopping time. At 2.0 m/s the field is 2.64 m short. All 143 runs fail. |
| TC-MUTE-001 | -3.77 s | No mute timeout is configured; a 4 s pick keeps the field muted for 5.77 s. |
| TC-SPEED-001 | -0.5925 m/s | Supervision acts only inside the zone. When navigation brakes late, the truck reaches 0.94 m/s inside a 0.3 m/s zone (permitted: 0.35). |
| TC-CROSS-001 | -0.548 m | A person stepping in about 1 m ahead is struck. Worst point: 2.0 m/s on a 0.10-friction floor; one failure is at 1.75 m/s on a 0.49 floor. 4 of 151 runs fail, all with the non-safety warning field off. |
| TC-CROSS-002 | -0.5423 m | The same from a cross aisle between racks, down to 1.5 m/s. 6 of 151 runs fail, all with the warning field off. |

The gate adds `R-PL-001` (Category 3 with medium DCavg gives PL d; HAZ-COLL-001 is
S2/F2/P2, so PLr e), `R-TRACE-002` (the reversing requirement SR-COLL-003 has no
test case), and `R-EXEC-002` for every test case.

The crossing counterexamples show why test cases sweep `warning_field` off. With the
non-safety slowdown on, rev A's worst crossing margin is +0.39 m; a campaign that left
it on would have credited a non-safety function with the safety argument.

### Rev B (`examples/amr_project_revb`)

The changes: a Category 4 architecture (DCavg high, giving PL e); a budget for the
declared worst floor (0.95 m/s² guaranteed at friction 0.10); maximum speed 1.0 m/s;
fields from the corrected budget; speed-margin field selection; ramp monitoring that
trips below the limit; a 1.8 s mute timeout; and a reversing test case.

1,262 SIL runs, 15.3 s. All six test cases pass. Smallest margins: a person stepping
in 1 m ahead of the truck at 1.0 m/s on the 0.10 floor, 0.056 m above the 0.10 m
separation (0.059 m from behind racks); speed-zone entry 0.055 m/s; reversing
0.098 m; mute 0.2 s.

The first rev B campaign did not pass, and the two failures were genuine lessons:

- Ramp monitoring aimed its braking curve at the zone limit itself, so a trip right
  at the boundary still overshot by the reaction time. The curve now targets the
  limit plus the (negative) trip offset.
- A reversing "collision" turned out to be a simulator artefact: pedestrians could
  walk through the stopped truck and reappear on the far side. They are now blocked
  by the body. The truck can still drive into them.
- An independent code review then found that rev B's negative zone trip offset also
  lowered the field-table overspeed trip, so the truck safe-stopped above 0.94 m/s.
  Rev B "passed" without ever cruising at the top of its tested speed range. The two
  offsets are now separate, and the numbers above come from the corrected model.
  A campaign can only falsify what the model lets happen.

The rev B gate still fails, on exactly six blockers: `R-EXEC-002` for every test
case, because simulation does not discharge a PL d/e verification obligation. That
is the intended end state. The design argument is complete, and physical
verification is the remaining work, which the tool will not let simulation stand in
for.

## 12. Scaling and known limits

| Dimension | Current | Path |
|---|---|---|
| Runs per campaign | 1,262 in 15 s on 16 processes (SIL) | shard by test case across machines, merge manifests by sequence |
| scenario_execution runner | about 2 s per run, of which about 1.8 s is `scenario_execution` parsing the OSC2 files; running it in-process was measured and is no faster | parse once per scenario file (needs `scenario_execution` internals) |
| Falsification | sequential per test case | parallel restarts |
| Trace storage | decimated x10, full resolution on failure | unchanged |
| STL `Until` | O(n^2) | rare in safety requirements |
| Evidence backend | filesystem, or S3-compatible object storage (tested on MinIO) | Object Lock retention; list-free head lookup for very long chains |
| HIL | protocol and emulator; no physical rig integration | one adapter per rig type |
| ROS 2 | plant node, rosbag2 recording and extraction, isolated containers | Gazebo / Nav2 plant with a site mapping |
| Multi-variant projects | one project per variant (rev A and rev B are two directories) | variant axis in `Project` |

### What this tool does **not** do

1. **It does not prove absence of violations.** Falsification is not verification.
   The technical file says so.
2. **It does not replace physical testing.** `R-EXEC-002` exists to stop that misuse.
3. **It does not validate your models.** The SIL world leaves out tyre slip, steering,
   sensor noise and reflectivity, pedestrians reacting, and controller faults
   (docs/SIMULATOR.md). Model validation is a separate obligation.
4. **It does not validate your ODD.** Coverage measures the *declared* space. In the
   examples, `entry_gap >= 1.0 m` is an assumption about the site, not a result.
5. **It does not read the standards for you.** Clause references are project data.
   The PL route is the simplified Annex K route.

## 13. Commercial read (hypotheses, not findings)

- **Deadline-driven demand.** Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 applies from 20 January 2027.
  AMR makers selling into the EU must reassess conformity under it, and anyone with
  ML in the safety path faces a Notified Body.
- **Asset-light.** Software, no hardware bill of materials.
- **The scarce skill is the evidence chain.** Automotive-grade V&V discipline mapped
  to ISO 3691-4 and ISO 13849 is a translation job. In structured warehouses,
  navigation and obstacle detection are widely available, and under ISO 13849 and
  3691-4 an ML perception stack cannot carry the safety function without the
  Notified Body route.

**Test before building further:** interview 8 to 10 AMR OEMs and integrators plus a
Notified Body about 2027 readiness and budget for validation tooling. Stop if fewer
than three name a budget.
