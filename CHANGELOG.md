# Changelog

## 0.4.0 (2026-09-15)

Items marked *(ran 0.3.0)* were reproduced by running the unmodified 0.3.0 code on
its own example; the numbers quoted come from those runs. The others were found by
reading the code. The named tests guard the fixes.

### Defects fixed

**Evidence integrity**

- *(ran 0.3.0)* **The report's Merkle root could never be verified.** `report` appended its own
  manifest entry under the campaign id after printing the root, which changed the
  root. On the shipped example the cover sheet said `2d3319ef...` and a recompute
  from the store gave `ce146787...`. Attestation entries are now outside the
  campaign root, and `safegate verify --report` checks both the root and the report
  file digest. Test: `test_report_root_is_recomputable_after_writing`.
- *(ran 0.3.0)* **Signatures were checked against a key stored next to the manifest.** Deleting
  the 386 failing runs from a 1,229-entry store, regenerating the key and
  re-signing gave a chain that `verify` accepted and a gate with zero R-EXEC-001
  findings. Keys can now live outside the store (`--signing-key`,
  `SAFEGATE_SIGNING_KEY`). The gate verifies against `trusted_signers` pinned in the
  policy and reports an unpinned chain as self-attested. `safegate anchor` and
  `verify --anchor` detect a rewrite of a published head. Test:
  `test_rewritten_chain_is_caught_by_pinned_key_and_anchor`.
- *(ran 0.3.0)* **A store with several campaigns was merged into one result.** After a second
  campaign on a new build ran a single test case, the gate and report still showed
  the 280 TC-CROSS-001 runs of the old build under the new build hash, which is
  threat T4. `load_campaign` reads exactly one campaign (latest, or `--campaign`).
  Test: `test_store_with_two_campaigns_reports_only_the_selected_one`.
- *(ran 0.3.0)* **Evidence tier came from the YAML, not the runner.** Runs were recorded at the
  tier the test case declared. Setting `tier: hil` on the closed-form NullRunner
  made its runs HIL evidence and removed all four R-EXEC-002 blockers. The tier is
  now the runner's, and the new R-EXEC-003 flags a declared tier the evidence does
  not meet. Test: `test_run_tier_comes_from_the_runner_not_the_yaml`.

**Reproducibility**

- *(ran 0.3.0)* **Campaigns differed between processes.** Seeds used Python's `hash(tc.ref)`,
  which is salted per process. Two CLI runs of the same campaign agreed on 25 of 86
  sampled points (only those that do not depend on the seed) and on 0 of 86 seeds. The in-process
  determinism test could not see it. Seeds now come from SHA-256. Test:
  `test_campaign_is_identical_across_processes`.
- *(ran 0.3.0)* The documented example numbers (936 runs, TC-CROSS-002 worst margin 2.227) did
  not come from the README command (`--sweep 256 --falsify 128` gives 1,222 runs)
  and could not be reproduced exactly because of the seed defect. All numbers in
  the docs are now generated by `scripts/reproduce.py`.

**The worked example**

- *(ran 0.3.0)* **The headline counterexample was a runner artefact.** NullRunner clipped the
  along-track distance at zero and detected people behind the truck. At the
  documented worst point (`truck_speed=2.0`, `detection_range=2.5`, ...), "contact"
  happened 12 s into the run with the truck 15.6 m past the crossing, when the
  pedestrian walked through the empty aisle behind it. With the geometry fixed, the
  same point gives a 4.24 m minimum distance and no braking. Test:
  `test_null_runner_does_not_count_a_person_behind_the_truck`.
- **TC-MUTE-001 contradicted its requirement.** SR-MUTE-001 allows muting for up to
  2.0 s, but the criterion `always protective_device_muted <= 0.5` failed on any
  muting, and the NullRunner's "muted" signal was actually the occlusion window. The
  reported counterexample (1.5 s) satisfied the requirement.
- **TC-SPEED-001 tested nothing.** NullRunner had no speed supervision, so every
  point with `truck_speed > zone_speed_limit` failed by construction.
- Three of the four scenario templates referenced by the test cases did not exist,
  and the loader did not check. `hazards.yaml` commented HAZ-COLL-001 as PLr d; the
  risk graph gives e (the report was right).

**Standards logic**

- **The ISO 13855 approach term covered only the latency.** With `human_approach`
  credited, the budget added `K * t_latency`. ISO 13855 defines T as the overall
  system response time up to cessation of the hazard, so a person keeps walking
  while the truck brakes: `K * (t_latency + v / a_brake)`. For the example budget,
  the required field at 1.5 m/s changes from 1.655 m to 3.654 m.
  `max_permitted_speed` and the vectorised derived signal use the same formula.
- **R-ML-001 was skipped whenever the PL determination was invalid**, which is the
  usual state of an ML architecture. It is now checked first. Test:
  `test_ml_rule_fires_even_when_the_determination_is_invalid`.
- **R-EXEC-002 keyed on the achieved PL only** and was skipped when the
  determination was invalid. The obligation is now the higher of required and
  achieved PL.
- **A test case with some errored runs was reported as PASS** (only all-errored
  gave ERROR). Any errored run now makes the verdict ERROR, with counts in the
  finding.

**Tooling**

- `ScenarioExecutionRunner` launched `scenario.osc` without ever writing it or the
  concrete parameters, so it could not run even with ROS installed.
- The CI workflow ran against a `safety/` directory that does not exist in this
  repository, and `safegate pl` returned 1 because of the deliberately invalid,
  unallocated ML architecture, which stopped the job before the campaign.
- The STL grammar documented function-call terms that the parser did not implement,
  and negative numbers did not parse.
- No lint configuration, and unused imports throughout. `ruff check src tests scripts` is
  now configured in pyproject.toml and clean.

### Found in an independent review of the 0.4.0 work, fixed before release

A fresh read-only review of the new code confirmed 15 defects. Several were in
code written for this release; the others were inherited from 0.3.0. Each has a
test in `tests/test_review_fixes.py`, `tests/test_stl.py` or `tests/test_evidence.py`.

- **Rev B's rated speed was never actually tested.** One offset served both the
  field-table overspeed trip and zone supervision. Rev B set it to -0.06 for zones,
  so the truck safe-stopped above 0.94 m/s in an empty aisle, and the top of the
  tested 0.3 to 1.0 m/s range never cruised. The offset is now split into
  `field_overspeed_trip_offset` and `zone_trip_offset`, and every rev B number was
  regenerated.
- **Repeats of non-deterministic runners.** The guard ran only when the first repeat
  had not errored, so the verdict depended on the order of repeats. `load_campaign`
  also counted every repeat as its own run, so the gate and report could disagree
  with the live campaign. The guard is now order-independent (any errored repeat
  means ERROR), repeats are tagged in the chain, and reading the chain back counts
  each point once.
- **R-EXEC-002 could be escaped** by a requirement that covers a PL d/e hazard but is
  listed by no safety function. The obligation now also comes from the hazards the
  test case's requirements cover, and the new blocker R-TRACE-004 flags such
  requirements.
- **Signing was not enforced.** With `require_signed_evidence: true`, an unsigned
  chain raised only a major finding, and without pinned keys no signature was
  checked at all. Unsigned entries and entries from more than one signer are now
  blockers. Every present signature is checked against the key the entry names,
  even without pinned keys.
- **An interrupted campaign could pass.** A campaign killed before its
  `campaign_end` entry was read back as if complete. Missing end and summary entries
  are now blockers.
- **A crashed worker lost a whole batch.** `pool.map` raised on the first dead
  worker and discarded results that had already finished. Jobs now complete
  individually; a job whose worker died is recorded as an ERROR run and the pool is
  restarted.
- **Empty or inverted STL intervals** (`always[2, 0.5] ...`) made `always` vacuously
  true. They are now rejected at parse time.
- **HIL build binding.** The campaign build hash came from `--build` even on a rig
  running different firmware. The rig's firmware digest is now the build hash, and a
  contradicting `--build` is refused.
- **Unbounded temporal operators** turned `b = inf` into the trace duration, and
  `t0 + (t_end - t0)` can round below `t_end` for traces that do not start at zero.
  This was not reproduced in 20,000 random traces, but infinity is now passed
  through unchanged.
- **Simulator timing.** Latencies were rounded to the nearest control period (for
  example 25 ms became 20 ms); they now round up. The mute timeout and restart delay
  compared float times and could run one step long; they now count integer steps. A
  reversal command flipped the velocity in one step; it now waits for standstill.
  The t=0 sample recorded placeholder zone and friction values.
- **`muted_elapsed` read one control period short** (N muted samples gave
  (N-1)·dt), the optimistic direction. It now uses sample-and-hold duration.
- **Merkle root.** An odd leaf was paired with itself, so `[a, b, c]` and
  `[a, b, c, c]` had the same root. Leaves and inner nodes now use distinct prefixes
  and odd nodes are promoted, as in RFC 6962.

### Found while completing the object-storage and ROS 2 work

- **Appends re-read the whole manifest** to find the chain head, so a campaign of
  N runs parsed about N^2/2 lines. The head is now read from the end of the log.
  Campaign wall time on the filesystem store went from 18.4 s to 4.7 s for rev A and
  from 53.0 s to 15.3 s for rev B, with identical results.
- **Concurrent writers could claim the same sequence number.** Two processes
  appending to one filesystem store raced between reading the head and writing.
  Appends now hold an exclusive lock for read, sign and write. The S3 backend uses
  conditional creates and retries on the new head. Tested with four concurrent
  processes on both.
- **`--store s3://...` silently wrote to a local directory named `s3:`**, because the
  CLI parsed the option as a filesystem path and collapsed the double slash. Stores
  are now plain strings. Test: `test_cli_keeps_object_storage_uris_intact`.
- **ReplayRunner could never select a recording.** It read a string `bag_id` from the
  run assignment, but assignments are numeric. Recordings are now selected by
  `bag_index` (sorted recording ids) or a numeric `bag_id`, and rosbag2 directories
  are extracted directly. Test: `test_replay_runner_reads_rosbag2_directly`.
- **The first campaign through ROS 2 came back FLAKY**, correctly. Parallel containers
  on Docker's default bridge network discovered each other over DDS multicast, so
  recorders captured other runs' plants. Containers now run with `--network none` and
  localhost-only discovery, and the repeats agree.
- `safegate run --runner hil` without `--rig-url` exited 1 instead of the usage-error
  code 2.

### Added

- **SIL world** (`safegate.sim`): deterministic 2D closed loop with truck footprint,
  floor-friction braking, ray-cast safety scanners with response time and tolerance,
  field-set switching, safe stop latency, zone speed supervision with optional ramp
  monitoring, muting with timeout, fallible non-safety navigation, and rack
  occlusion. Six scenario families. `SilRunner` is the default runner. See
  docs/SIMULATOR.md.
- **OpenSCENARIO 2 through Intel Labs `scenario_execution` 1.5.0**, using its
  step-based `SimulationInterface`: the `osc.safegate` action library, six scenario
  files, per-run parameter files, and JUnit failure parsing. Every family is
  bit-identical to the in-process runner (tests), and so is a full campaign
  (docs/RESULTS.md).
- **HIL rig protocol** `safegate-hil/1` with contract enforcement in `HilRunner`
  (complete pinning, scanner checksum, no mid-campaign changes, only
  `physical: true` rigs yield HIL tier), plus an emulator for contract tests. See
  docs/HIL_PROTOCOL.md.
- **Derived signals** (`required_field_length`, `muted_elapsed`,
  `separation_while_moving`) so requirements can reference the stopping budget in
  plain STL.
- **Linear STL expressions**, for example `speed <= zone_speed_limit + 0.05`.
- Gate rules **R-EXEC-003** (declared tier met), **R-TRACE-004** (requirements
  realised by a safety function) and **R-EVID-002** (evidence
  produced against the current design data and the expected build). Campaigns
  record the project digest, budgets, seed and SafeGate version.
- CLI: `keygen`, `anchor`, `verify --report/--anchor/--trusted-key`,
  `run --workers/--signing-key/--runner {sil,scenario_execution,hil,null}`,
  `gate --json`, and `--campaign` selection on run, gate, report and anchor.
- Parallel campaign execution with a deterministic recording order. Test:
  `test_parallel_workers_record_the_same_evidence`.
- **Example T200 rev B** (`examples/amr_project_revb`): the design after the rev A
  findings, with a Category 4 architecture, a budget for the worst floor in the ODD,
  a 1.0 m/s maximum speed, fields from the corrected budget, ramp monitoring, a mute
  timeout, and a reversing test case. It discharges everything simulation can; its
  only remaining blockers are the HIL obligations.
- **Object-storage evidence backend** (`core/storage.py`, extra `s3`): `--store
  s3://bucket/prefix`, one object per manifest entry created with
  `If-None-Match: *`, content-addressed blobs, tested against MinIO including
  concurrent writers and the full CLI pipeline.
- **ROS 2 path**: `safegate.ros.plant` (the SIL world as a ROS 2 Jazzy node publishing
  `sensor_msgs/JointState`), `safegate.ros.record_scenario` (rosbag2 MCAP recording),
  `docker/ros2/Dockerfile`, `--runner ros2`, and `safegate.execution.rosbag`, a
  mapping-driven rosbag2 extractor (MCAP and SQLite3, extra `ros`) that runs without a
  ROS installation. A ROS 2 campaign reproduces the in-process results exactly.
- `scripts/reproduce.py`, `scripts/make_results.py` and `scripts/bench.py`;
  docs/RESULTS.md and docs/reports/ are generated.
- `.github/workflows/ci.yml` for this repository; the adopter template moved to
  `docs/ci/safety-gate.yml`.

### Changed

- The loader rejects missing scenario templates and STL criteria that do not parse.
- `safegate pl` exits non-zero only for architectures allocated to a safety
  function.
- NullRunner publishes only the signals it models (no muting, no zone limit), so a
  criterion over an unmodelled signal errors instead of passing vacuously.
- Unbounded `always`/`eventually` is vectorised; on the benchmark machine a
  10,000-sample `always` went from 9.6 ms to 0.05 ms. Bounded windows are
  unchanged.
- Long dashes removed from code, docs and generated reports.
